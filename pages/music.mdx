# Notes about music
2025-10-26T15:19:54-04:00

Like many people I have had a lifelong relationship with music. My father blasted 60s and 70s hits on the living room hi-fi throughout my childhood, which I mostly found obnoxious but a few gems have managed to stick with me. Mostly it's been late 90s video game soundtracks that have continued to be the most influential on me.

The earliest albums I owned were Gordon Lightfoot's _Summertime Dream_ and Green Day's _International Superhits_ on CD, which I listened to on a Sony portable before quickly graduating to a Panasonic MP3 player (and later a number of early-generation iPods).

## Music Education

In my last year of elementary, my school was just starting a new elective music program. We'd had a regular music class previously, but partway through fifth grade we had the option of joining a concert band which would provide instruments and teach us to read music and play in ensemble. I went in with the intention of playing the flute but never managed to make a sound on the mouthpiece. I sometimes wonder how different my career could have been had anyone told me to blow across the opening rather than directly into it... Nonetheless, percussion served me well over the next ten years despite feeling like the loser option at the time. I eagerly dug into whatever I could get my hands on including jazz band, marching band, orchestra, and my own short-lived band called _Braintap_.

### Other Favorites

*Jami Sieber*:
A brilliant electric cello player with a span of solo albums. Discovered her through the _Braid_ soundtrack in 2008 and she has since become a staple for my river trail runs.

*Caroline Polachek*:
An exceptional vocalist and synth pop artist, previously known as part of the indie band Chairlift. 
I first heard her on NPR's Tiny Desk. Her solo vocal work is enchanting, and while I'm not typically a fan of electronic effects I find hers are very tasteful and don't detract at all from her performance.

*Rhiannon Mair*:
The vocalist whom I consider my all-time favorite sadly stopped recording and pulled almost all her original work after a relatively short career. Rhiannon Mair is a music producer who released several group and solo albums around the early 2010s. I would characterize her music as UK pop with some folk and reggae influence (usually in DADGAD or similar alt tunings). You can no longer hear it anywhere apart from a small handful of official tracks and some scattered sessions left on YouTube. The backups I've managed to keep are a treasure.

*Postmodern Jukebox*:
Pop hits reworked into vintage genres, performed by a large rotation of singers and instrumentalists. As of 2025 they publish on YouTube roughly once a month.

*Charles Berthoud*:
A titan of a bassist, master of every technique under the sun. He does largely solo instrumental work including original music videos and other content on YouTube.
