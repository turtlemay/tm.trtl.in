# Thoughts on Android Auto
2025-10-10T04:53:15-04:00

As a scooter delivery boy, I navigate every day using a cheap Android phone which is frequently docked and undocked over the course of my shift, alternating between my map and delivery app. My thinking as I initially looked into Android Auto was that having a permanent map on the dashboard would ease both of those conditions, allowing me to keep my phone on me, conserve battery, and have my main app already open when needed.

This is ostensibly the purpose of AA, although it's hard to really discern that or anything else about it due to lack of documentation or any sane marketing of the feature. Is it an OS? Is it a protocol? Is it an application? Even after using it I couldn't say for sure. From what I can gather, the entire AA interface must be processed behind the scenes on the Android device and rendered out like a secondary monitor. Only select apps that have developed an AA interface can be used.

I tried a couple of low-end motorcycle-oriented displays (the former having an annoying UI glitch that would cause the menu to flicker on and off repeatedly and the latter often failing to connect to the phone). Glitches aside, the general experience was powering on the vehicle, waiting several seconds for the phone to pair, then the display automatically renders whatever route you're running on Google Maps.

Problems start when you need to change routes, which can effectively only be done from the phone, at least without copious extra steps. Then I realized the phone needed to be docked a majority of the time anyway due to the nature of how most of my orders are assigned while driving, which largely negates the original purpose. The final nail in the coffin was realizing that AA uses its own wifi network to transfer data, preventing data hotspot usage which puts a damper on other parts of my workflow.

In short, I found the Android Auto platform as a whole to be inefficient, not supported by the vast majority of apps, and having little benefit compared to the hardware cost.
