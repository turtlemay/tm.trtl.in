# My experience with computers
2025-10-10T07:17:08-04:00

I was born in the mid-90s and grew up with a Windows 95 Packard Bell family computer. Without home internet, I spent lots of time tinkering with Microsoft Office and Paint, playing DOS games like Super Solvers and Chip's Challenge, and reading Compton's Interactive Encyclopedia. Floppy disks were already on the way out but got me by for school projects, usually printed off at the public library or in some cases transferred to CD-ROM for those newer computers without an A drive.[^usbdrive] The school computers were already on Office XP ahead of me upgrading to Windows 98 at home, so working out the file formats was one way I was forced to develop some software literacy early on.

[^usbdrive]: I bought my first 1GB flash drive from Target in 2005, used for my PortableApps suite and showing off my random files and screenshots in the computer lab.

I learned touch typing and basic web development in my 4th and 5th grade computer classes. I started at about 20wpm and thought I would never touch my friend Phong's god-tier 35wpm. We learned a lot of interesting stuff in our last year like making games and animation using PowerPoint. Phong was always ahead of the curve, like being the first one to have his own published website which he showed off one day on the classroom projector.

From 2005 I used a Windows XP Media Center PC with a broadband connection, which gave me access to free software like GIMP, Google Sketchup, Noteworthy Composer, Audacity, VirtualDub, Visual Basic, XAMPP, and tons of other programs discovered through tech blogs, freeware sites, open-source repositories such as SourceForge, and early tech YouTubers such as Chris Pirillo and Jimmy Ruska. Soon after I got my first laptop I started experimenting with a variety of Linux distributions, learning programming concepts, and diving deeper into web development as a creative outlet. This was a prolific time for me, when I learned as much as I could about different computer-related industries. The web was still evolving as a platform for information and creativity and the software industry had not yet shifted toward cloud services, social media, and smart devices. Sort of a golden age in computing that I know I'm not alone in yearning for.

Today I do most of my computing on a low-end Chromebook logged into my home Ubuntu server terminal. Only music and video editing are still handled by a Windows workstation.
